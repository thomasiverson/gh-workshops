GitHub Copilot Administration Workshop

Duration: 60 minutes, including demos, discussion, and closing Q&A; no break
Format: Instructor-led presentation + safe live demos in the instructor's environment
Audience: Customer GitHub Copilot administrators, enterprise and organization owners, and endpoint and billing administrators
Focus: AI controls and governance, client configuration through managed-settings.json, and budgeting and administrative operations
Delivery readiness: Instructor environment rehearsal required for live demos; offline alternatives are provided
Product-source review date: October 28, 2026

Workshop Overview

Administer Copilot as a managed service: decide what is allowed, deliver client guardrails, verify the effective configuration, and control consumption. Participants observe the instructor and answer short administrative scenarios; they do not need an administrator account or a configured device.

This is a standalone workshop, not a module of the AI Credits curriculum. It has its own one-hour scope and current product-source baseline. Longer billing exercises are available in the administration and budget guardrails module.

Learning Objectives

By the end, administrators can:

  1. Assign an administrative requirement to the correct control plane and explain enterprise enforcement versus organization delegation.
  2. Read a valid managed-settings.json example, choose a delivery method, and verify an effective setting on a supported client.
  3. Distinguish seat charges, included AI credits, user-level budgets, and metered spending limits.
  4. Diagnose a blocked-user scenario and name the owner, evidence, and recovery path.

1. Opening and administrator responsibilities (5 min)

Key Points

Use the first two minutes for introductions and objectives, then spend three minutes establishing responsibility and the safety boundary.

  • Access determines who receives Copilot; policy determines which capabilities are available. Enabling a feature is not the same as assigning a license.
  • Client guardrails govern supported local behavior; billing controls govern consumption and paid usage.
  • A setting being visible in a portal does not prove that the correct account, device, client, or billing scope receives it.
  • Recommendations in this workshop are operating practices, not mandatory GitHub defaults or contractual guarantees.

Administrative Ownership

Responsibility Operational owner to nominate Evidence to retain
Service policies and license lifecycle Enterprise or organization administration Approved scope, policy state, license source, change record
Managed client rollout Endpoint/platform administration Reviewed JSON, deployment target, client version, effective-setting check
Consumption and exceptions Billing administration with FinOps Billing entity, budget type/scope, hard-stop state, approver
Data handling and incidents Security/privacy team with service owner Approved use cases, control limitations, escalation and containment plan

These are recommended responsibilities, not a complete GitHub permission matrix. Enterprise owners or users with Manage enterprise AI controls can manage enterprise policies. Billing roles are separate; an endpoint administrator does not automatically have GitHub billing rights. See policy permissions and budget roles.

Discussion Points

  • Who owns service policy, endpoint deployment, and billing in your customer environment? Identify a missing handoff rather than expecting one administrator to own everything.

2. AI controls and governance (15 min)

Key Points

Allocate 7 minutes to the control model, 5 minutes to Demo 1, and 3 minutes to the decision scenario and discussion.

Choose the Correct Control Plane

Requirement Primary control plane Verification evidence
Decide which Copilot clients, features, and models licensed users can access GitHub enterprise/organization AI controls Policy scope and effective account entitlement
Apply supported Copilot behavior across clients Copilot enterprise-managed settings Delivered key, supported client, effective value and source
Manage VS Code-specific editor behavior VS Code device policies Device deployment and Policy Diagnostics
Limit AI credit consumption or additional spend GitHub billing controls Billing entity, budget scope, consumption, hard-stop state

Repository instructions and prompts provide guidance; they are not an enforceable administrative security boundary.

Policy Scope and Delegation

  • Navigate to Enterprise > AI controls. Copilot contains administration, privacy, model, billing, and usage controls; Configure features & clients exposes the relevant client controls. Agents and MCP have separate areas.
  • An enterprise can enforce a policy or, for most policies, let organizations decide. Organization settings cannot undo enterprise enforcement.
  • Users assigned a license directly by the enterprise require attention to Policies for enterprise-assigned users; organization delegation alone does not define their default.
  • Do not assume one universal enterprise-to-organization-to-repository hierarchy for every control. Scope and supported surfaces vary.
  • Multiple license sources can change policy resolution. Confirm the user's license and billing source instead of diagnosing from one organization page.
  • Review defaults for newly available GA features and models. A decision about today's model catalog does not settle tomorrow's default availability.

Sources: policy concepts, enterprise configuration, and supported policy surfaces.

Governance Decisions to Record

Decision Recommended administrative practice Limitation to communicate
Features, models, and preview access Assign an approver and a review cadence; pilot changes Availability and policy coverage differ by feature and client
MCP and agent tools Review the server, identity, permissions, and downstream data destination A Copilot policy is not blanket governance for every third-party host
Public-code matching Set the applicable policy with legal/IP stakeholders Filtering does not replace code review or license analysis
Content exclusion Use supported exclusions where appropriate; verify the exact surface Not supported in editor Edit/Agent modes; not a universal DLP boundary
Policy changes and incidents Keep approved baselines and audit evidence; scope containment to the issue Do not promise that every Copilot activity or prompt appears in the audit log

Content exclusion can also receive indirect semantic information from an IDE; do not demonstrate it as proof that no sensitive information can ever reach a model. Consult the current content exclusion limitations. Refer data retention, residency, and contractual questions to current product and legal documentation rather than making blanket promises.

Demo 1: Inspect AI Controls and Effective Scope (5 min)

Follow Demo 1 in the runbook.

  1. Confirm the non-production enterprise and test identity.
  2. Inspect an enforced policy and an organization-delegated policy.
  3. Show the organization view and explain which decision it can make.
  4. Identify the owner and evidence required for an approved change; do not save anything.

Checkpoint: Attendees identify the policy's scope, enforcement/delegation state, and a supported client. A disabled organization control inherited from the enterprise is expected behavior, not a portal defect.

Discussion Points

  • A team requests a new model and an external MCP server. Which approvals are separate, and what data flow must be reviewed?
  • Your enterprise delegates a policy, but a directly assigned user sees different behavior. What account and assignment evidence would you check first?

3. Client configuration with managed-settings.json (20 min)

Key Points

Allocate 8 minutes to schema and delivery, 7 minutes to Demo 2, and 5 minutes to troubleshooting and discussion.

Enterprise-managed settings centrally configure supported Copilot client behavior. The documented clients include Copilot CLI, VS Code, the GitHub Copilot app, Copilot cloud agent, and JetBrains IDEs, but not every key works in every client. Check the per-key reference, not just the client list.

Read the Example

The copyable template contains:

{
  "model": "auto",
  "permissions": {
    "disableBypassPermissionsMode": "disable"
  }
}
  • model is a top-level default for new conversations, not a model allowlist. Users can select another permitted model for a conversation.
  • permissions.disableBypassPermissionsMode takes the string "disable", not a Boolean. In Copilot CLI it blocks bypass/allow-all modes. For VS Code, confirm the exact client, harness, effective policy, and permission UI during rehearsal.
  • Disabling bypass does not disable assisted permissions or guarantee a prompt for every operation. Use separately supported permission controls if the requirement is fresh approval for particular operations.
  • The example is deliberately small. It does not configure sandboxing, network restrictions, exclusions, or budgets.

VS Code documentation discrepancy: The GitHub reference describes the bypass key as disabling global auto-approval. The VS Code guide describes removing Allow all from the Copilot session permissions picker and treats the ChatToolsAutoApprove global device policy separately. Do not equate these controls or attribute an independently enforced restriction to the sample. Resolve the exact behavior through authorized client rehearsal or vendor clarification before making a live enforcement claim; otherwise use the labeled illustrative fallback.

Delivery Methods

Method Configuration and targeting Administrative trade-off
Server-managed Selected .github-private repository; default-branch file copilot/managed-settings.json; enterprise-licensed users Reviewable history; supports enterprise-team overrides; also reaches supported cloud clients
Native MDM OS-managed key/string values; Windows registry or macOS managed preferences Device targeting; local clients only; not a JSON file copied to a registry key
File-based Protected platform-specific managed-settings.json on each target environment Local clients only; useful in VMs/containers; devices without the file do not receive that control

For server delivery, the repository must be selected under Enterprise > AI controls > Agents > Configuration source. Creating a similarly named repository alone does not activate governance. Default-branch changes apply to enterprise-licensed users in scope, even when they cannot read that repository.

On Windows, file delivery uses:

%ProgramFiles%\GitHubCopilot\managed-settings.json

Native Windows MDM uses string (REG_SZ) values under:

HKEY_LOCAL_MACHINE\SOFTWARE\Policies\GitHubCopilot

For that MDM method, use the dotted key and string value:

permissions.disableBypassPermissionsMode = disable

That notation explains a native policy entry; it is not JSON or a command. Booleans, arrays, and objects in native MDM are stored as JSON text inside string values. Check the deployment guide for macOS/Linux locations and permissions. Do not place the Copilot file in the VS Code device-policy namespace or assume workspace settings enforce it.

Precedence and Operational Limits

  • General delivery precedence: MDM-managed > server-managed > file-based > user-level settings.
  • Exceptions compose restrictively: sandbox and permission rule arrays are not ordinary last-writer-wins settings. A bypass disable from any managed layer cannot be relaxed by a lower layer.
  • For an overlapping VS Code device-policy control, the Copilot enterprise-managed setting takes precedence; configure one authoritative system per overlapping control when practical.
  • Server changes normally reach supported clients within about an hour; restarting or signing in again triggers refresh. File updates require client restart. Stage changes before the workshop.
  • If CLI cannot fetch server settings and has no cached response, those server settings are unavailable for that session. For controls that must exist before sign-in or without a server response, evaluate MDM/file delivery and the documented fresh-policy options.
  • In VS Code, applying enterprise-managed settings disables the Claude and Codex harness integrations because those harnesses cannot enforce these controls. This is a harness restriction, not a claim that Claude models are unavailable in Copilot.
  • Adding advanced keys can have version or host prerequisites and can block users on unsupported clients. Do not copy a large policy sample without testing it.

Sources: deployment and precedence, setting semantics, and VS Code management layers.

Demo 2: Inspect Managed Settings on a Client (7 min)

Follow Demo 2 in the runbook. Prefer a pre-staged disposable Windows VM using file delivery so enterprise-wide changes are unnecessary.

  1. Read the local template and identify the two settings.
  2. Inspect the pre-staged file and its protected location.
  3. Run Developer: Policy Diagnostics in VS Code and inspect the effective managed bypass restriction, its source, and the matching permission UI.
  4. Explain the model default versus the service model policy without sending a prompt.

Checkpoint: Evidence identifies the effective managed bypass restriction and its source on the actual client and harness. An unrelated disabled global-auto-approval control, parsed JSON, or a file on disk alone does not prove enforcement by the sample. Sanitize diagnostics before screen sharing or retaining them.

Troubleshooting Sequence

  1. Confirm client/version, signed-in account, license source, and intended billing entity.
  2. Check that the key is supported for that client and execution mode.
  3. Check source selection, protected file location, native value types, and higher-precedence settings.
  4. Validate JSON and inspect GitHub's server-settings validation when using repository delivery; inspect the client's effective configuration.
  5. Refresh/restart as documented; test on the disposable device. If the checkpoint still fails, report it and use the offline explanation rather than weakening a policy.

Discussion Points

  • A developer can change a normal editor preference but cannot select a restricted session permission level. What evidence identifies the managed control and its source?
  • Your file says one value and diagnostics shows another source. Which delivery channel would you inspect before editing the file again?

4. Budgeting and administrative operations (15 min)

Key Points

Allocate 6 minutes to the billing model, 5 minutes to Demo 3, and 4 minutes to the scenario and operating cadence.

Separate Licenses, Credits, and Paid Usage

  • Seat charges cover assigned Copilot licenses. Current published Business pricing is $19 USD/user/month with 1,900 included AI credits; Enterprise is $39 USD/user/month with 3,900. Confirm the customer's contract and current pricing.
  • AI credits measure usage; 1 credit equals $0.01 USD. Included credits are pooled at the billing entity, reset monthly at 00:00 UTC on the first day, and do not roll over.
  • Model and token consumption drive AI credit usage. Code completions and next edit suggestions do not consume AI credits and remain available when an AI-credit budget blocks credit-consuming features.
  • AI credits paid usage controls usage beyond the included allowance; GitHub documents additional usage as enabled by default. Inspect the actual customer setting rather than relying on that default.
  • A metered spending budget does not cap license charges. Removing a seat does not refund the remainder of the current billing cycle.

Sources: seats and billing cycles and usage-based billing.

Budget Controls Are Not Interchangeable

Control What it governs Blocking behavior
User-level budget (ULB) One user's total credit consumption, including included and metered usage Always a hard stop; $0 blocks that user immediately
Cost-center, organization, or enterprise spending budget Metered charges, not the seat bill or ordinary included-pool consumption Stops usage only when the applicable hard-stop option is enabled
AI credits paid usage policy Whether usage can continue as paid usage beyond included credits If disabled, exhaustion blocks credit-consuming features
Budget alerts Notification about the selected spending scope Notifications alone do not stop usage

Within user-level budgets, the most specific value wins: individual > cost-center user-level > universal. This is replacement, not addition. Cost-center user-level budgets are different from cost-center spending budgets.

Keep the one-hour explanation bounded:

  • Inspect all applicable scopes; another exhausted hard limit can block a user with ULB headroom.
  • Cost-center included usage controls are a separate option that can cap that cost center's draw from included credits; they are not its metered spending budget.
  • Cost-center exclusion can remove that cost center's metered charges from the enterprise spending cap. Never present an enterprise cap as an unconditional cap on every team's spend.
  • Budget alerts can be enabled at 75%, 90%, and 100% for supported scopes. User-level alerting is not consistently available; do not depend on it alone.
  • New spending budgets measure metered usage from their creation onward, not earlier spending in that cycle.
  • A budget block does not automatically switch the user to a cheaper model.

Sources: Copilot budget controls and budgets and alerts.

Worked Example: Plan the Bill, Then Diagnose the Block

Illustrative full-month configuration: 100 Business seats at public list price, one billing entity, a pre-existing $300 Copilot metered hard-stop budget, no excluded cost centers, no other charges, taxes, discounts, or proration.

Component Calculation Result
Monthly seat charges 100 x $19 $1,900
Included shared AI credits 100 x 1,900 190,000 credits
Approved metered usage allowance $300 / $0.01 30,000 additional credits
Planned license-plus-metered ceiling for this simplified configuration $1,900 + $300 $2,200

This is a planning example, not a quote or a guarantee for other billing configurations.

Scenario: A user has consumed $20 worth of AI credits and reached an individual $20 ULB. The shared pool still has credits, and the enterprise spending budget has headroom.

Answer: The user is blocked from credit-consuming features by the individual ULB. Raising the enterprise spending budget does not fix that limit. An authorized administrator reviews the effective ULB and consumption, then approves a scoped adjustment or waits for reset. Do not raise limits just to demonstrate recovery. Code completions and next edit suggestions continue.

Demo 3: Inspect Budgets and Identify the Limiting Control (5 min)

Follow Demo 3 in the runbook.

  1. Open the instructor's non-production Billing & Licensing > Budgets and alerts page.
  2. Inspect the pre-existing budget's type, scope, amount, hard-stop state, and alert recipients.
  3. Contrast spending evidence with license/activity evidence.
  4. Resolve the worked blocked-user example verbally. Do not create a budget or consume credits.

Checkpoint: Participants name the actual limiting control and its administrative owner, rather than assuming an alert, a seat change, or a larger enterprise budget solves every block.

Recommended Operating Cadence

Cadence Administrative action
Onboarding/offboarding Confirm license source, identity, approved clients, endpoint policy, and removal workflow
Weekly during rollout Review usage/blocked-user patterns, alerts, failed deployments, and support tickets
Monthly Reconcile assigned seats, activity, included credits, metered charges, and cost-center membership; review exceptions
On change or incident Record scope, approver, current baseline, rollback owner, validation evidence, and developer communication

Usage/adoption reports are not invoices, and activity is not a standalone productivity score. Avoid displaying customer names or exporting reports during training. For time-limited exceptions, record an expiry and owner; use a supported individual-budget expiration where available.

Discussion Points

  • A user is blocked while the shared pool still has credits. Which control should you inspect before approving more paid usage?
  • Who receives a spending alert, and who can approve an exception before a critical team is affected?

5. Administrator checklist and Q&A (5 min)

Key Points

Use two minutes for a three-question teach-back, two minutes for Q&A, and one minute for next actions.

Teach-back question Expected answer
Where do you allow a Copilot client or model? Applicable GitHub AI controls, with license/account scope verified
Does copying JSON prove the device is governed? No; verify supported keys, actual delivery, and effective client evidence
Does a spending alert stop usage or cap seat fees? No; inspect hard-stop behavior, scope, and separate license charges

Customer Takeaway Checklist

  • Name the service-policy, endpoint, billing, and incident owners.
  • Inventory license sources, client versions, allowed features/models, and exceptions.
  • Record enforced/delegated decisions and defaults for new features/models.
  • Choose a managed-settings delivery method; pilot with representative accounts and devices.
  • Retain effective-setting evidence, not just deployment success.
  • Confirm paid-usage policy, ULBs, spending scopes, hard stops, alert recipients, and excluded cost centers.
  • Define a blocked-user escalation path and time-bounded exception approval.
  • Schedule recurring seat, usage, policy-drift, and budget reviews.

Discussion Points

  • Which one control or handoff will you verify first after this session, and who will own it?
