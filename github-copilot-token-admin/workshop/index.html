Workshop Overview

GitHub Copilot billing for Business and Enterprise plans is usage-based, measured in GitHub AI Credits. Before any developer optimization conversation makes sense, the people who own budgets — enterprise/org owners, billing managers, FinOps leads, and platform admins — need a precise, accurate mental model of how credits are consumed, pooled, and capped, and how to configure guardrails before a rollout scales. This module gives that audience a hands-on budget-configuration exercise, the exact billing-flow mechanics, and a repeatable administrative operating procedure to run every month.

Note: All facts, pricing, and screenshots referenced in this workshop reflect GitHub documentation as of 2026-08-08. Pricing and included-credit amounts change over time — always verify against the live docs linked in the Appendix before delivering this workshop.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain the AI Credits billing model: the credit-to-dollar conversion, included monthly allotments, pooling, and monthly reset behavior
  • Distinguish which Copilot features consume AI credits and which do not
  • Configure and interpret budgets at the universal, cost-center, individual-user, organization, and enterprise levels — including precedence and hard-stop behavior
  • Trace a Copilot request through the full billing flow: user budget check → shared pool → additional usage policy → applicable spending budget/hard stop
  • Distinguish a cost center's included-usage allocation from a cost center's spending budget
  • Draft a monthly administrative runbook covering alerts, reviews, and an exception/escalation path
  • Identify what changes for existing customers when the June–September 2026 promotional credit window ends

1. AI Credits Billing Fundamentals (20 min)

Key Points

  • GitHub AI Credits are the billing unit for Copilot Business and Enterprise: 1 AI credit = $0.01 USD
  • Every Copilot Business license includes 1,900 AI credits/user/month; every Copilot Enterprise license includes 3,900 AI credits/user/month
  • Credits are pooled at the billing entity level — a heavy user in one team can draw on a lighter user's unused share
  • The pool resets at 00:00:00 UTC on the first day of each month; unused credits do not roll over
  • Code completions and next-edit suggestions (NES) remain unlimited on paid plans and are not billed in AI credits
  • AI-credit-billed features include: Copilot Chat, Copilot CLI, Copilot cloud agent, Copilot Spaces, and third-party coding agents
  • Additional usage (paid overage/metered billing) is enabled by default for organizations and enterprises — an admin must explicitly disable the AI credits paid usage policy to prevent any spend beyond included credits

Important — Promotional period ends 2026-09-01: Existing Business/Enterprise customers receive a higher promotional allotment from June 1 – September 1, 2026: 3,000 credits/user/month (Business) and 7,000 credits/user/month (Enterprise). Starting September 1, 2026, allotments return to the standard 1,900 / 3,900 amounts. Use the promotional window to baseline usage and size budgets before the pool shrinks — do not treat the promotional numbers as your permanent capacity.

Included Credits at a Glance

Plan Standard (from Sep 1, 2026) Promo (Jun 1 – Sep 1, 2026)
Business 1,900/user/month 3,000/user/month
Enterprise 3,900/user/month 7,000/user/month

2. Budget Controls & Billing Flow (25 min)

Key Points

  • Budgets are the tool that governs both scenarios from Section 1 — capping an individual's draw from the pool, and capping paid overage once the pool is exhausted
  • There are three types of user-level budgets (ULBs), evaluated most-specific-wins:
ULB Type Scope Precedence
Universal ULB Every licensed user in the enterprise (default) Lowest — overridden by the other two
Cost-center ULB Every member of one cost center Overrides universal for that cost center's members
Individual ULB One specific user Highest — overrides both of the above

Quick reference: Open the two-slide Power-User Budget Override visual for a worked $30 → $75 → $125 example showing how an individual ULB replaces broader limits.

  • ULBs cap a user's total consumption (pool + metered) and always hard-stop — a $0 ULB blocks that user immediately, regardless of pool or policy state
  • Cost-center, organization, and enterprise budgets only cap metered (paid overage) charges, and only take effect after the shared pool is exhausted
  • Those higher-level budgets hard-stop only if "Stop usage when budget limit is reached" is enabled — this is off by default, meaning an unconfigured spending budget is an alert, not a guardrail
  • There is no automatic fallback to a cheaper model when a budget is exhausted — the user is simply blocked
  • Cost centers have two distinct controls, not one: a cost center's included-usage allocation is simply the sum of its members' included pooled credits (part of the shared-pool phase); a cost-center budget is a separate spending-limit construct that only governs the metered phase after that allocation is exhausted. Sizing one does not automatically size the other

Enterprise Teams as the Membership Layer

Enterprise teams provide a scalable way to align identity, Copilot access, and cost attribution without maintaining individual user lists in multiple places.

Capability Administrative Impact
Assign an enterprise team to a cost center Every current team member is added to the cost center, and cost-center membership updates automatically as people join or leave the team
Assign Copilot Business licenses directly Copilot access follows enterprise-team membership independently of organization membership
Sync an enterprise team with an IdP group (EMU) Membership changes flow from the identity provider through SCIM; an IdP-managed enterprise team cannot also contain manually assigned users
Enforce single cost-center ownership An organization, repository, user, or enterprise team can belong to only one cost center; assigning it elsewhere moves it to the new cost center

Important: Enterprise teams automate who belongs to a cost center; they do not automatically size the cost-center ULB, included-usage control, or metered spending budget. Administrators must still review those controls as membership changes.

Budget Control Comparison

Control What It Caps When Active Scope Hard Stop?
Universal user-level budget Each user's total AI credit consumption Always (pool + metered) Per user Always
Cost-center user-level budget Each member's total consumption (overrides universal) Always (pool + metered) Per user, by cost center Always
Individual user-level budget A specific user's total consumption (overrides both above) Always (pool + metered) Per user Always
Cost-center budget A team's metered charges after its included-usage allocation is exhausted Metered phase only Per cost center Only if hard stop enabled
Organization budget An org's metered charges after pool exhaustion Metered phase only Per organization Only if hard stop enabled
Enterprise budget Total enterprise metered charges after pool exhaustion Metered phase only Enterprise-wide Only if hard stop enabled

Important: The enterprise budget is not your total monthly bill. Your maximum possible spend is license fees + the enterprise budget. Example: 400 Copilot Business licenses at $19/month = $7,600 in license fees. A $5,000 enterprise budget means your maximum bill is $12,600 — not $5,000.

Alerts

Alert Type Thresholds
Included-usage alerts Fire at 90% and 100% of included/pooled credits
Budget alerts Fire at 75%, 90%, and 100% of the configured budget amount

Billing Flow Diagram

The diagram below traces a single Copilot request through every decision point, from the user's own budget through the shared pool to any applicable spending budget. This diagram must remain identical across this workshop, the LAB guide, and the companion slide deck.

┌────────────────┐ ┌────────────────┐ ┌────────────────┐ ┌────────────────┐ ┌────────────────┐
│      User      │ │  User budget   │ │     Shared     │ │   Paid usage   │ │Spending budget │
│    request     │►│     check      │►│      pool      │►│     policy     │►│  / hard stop   │
└────────────────┘ └────────────────┘ └────────────────┘ └────────────────┘ └────────────────┘

Decision points to walk through out loud, stage by stage:

  1. User budget check — the most specific active ULB (individual > cost-center > universal) is evaluated first. A $0 or exhausted ULB always blocks, regardless of pool or policy state — there is no fallback to a cheaper model.
  2. Shared pool — if the user's ULB has room, the request draws from the pooled AI credits (org/enterprise, resets monthly at 00:00 UTC, no rollover). While the pool has credits, the request is served with no additional metered charge beyond the license fee.
  3. Paid usage policy — once the pool is exhausted, the "AI credits paid usage" policy (enabled by default) decides whether metered usage even begins. If disabled, the user is blocked until the next billing cycle.
  4. Spending budget / hard stop — if metered usage is allowed, the applicable spending budget (cost-center, organization, or enterprise) determines whether the charge goes through. It only hard-stops if "Stop usage when budget limit is reached" was explicitly enabled (off by default) — otherwise the charge is billed as overage and only an alert fires.

🧪 Hands-On: Scenario Trace (8 min)

Roles: Admin and Observer work together; either role can lead the trace out loud.

Using the diagram above, trace what happens in each scenario and write down where the request is blocked (if at all) and why:

  1. A user with a $0 individual ULB sends a Chat prompt. The org pool still has 40% of its credits remaining.
  2. A user with no individual override sends a prompt. The shared pool is fully exhausted, and the enterprise has disabled the "AI credits paid usage" policy.
  3. A user with no individual override sends a prompt. The shared pool is exhausted, paid usage is enabled, and the enterprise budget has hit 100% with hard stop not enabled.
  4. Same as #3, but hard stop is enabled on the enterprise budget.

  • ✅ You correctly identify Scenario 2 as blocked at the paid usage policy step

  • ✅ You correctly identify Scenario 4 as blocked at the spending budget step

3. Hands-On Budget Configuration (30 min)

Key Points

  • Getting-started recommendation for a first budget rollout:
    1. Set a universal ULB above the per-license value — $19 for Business, $39 for Enterprise. Setting it above the license value lets pooling work as intended: heavier users draw from lighter users' unused share instead of being capped at exactly their own license cost.
    2. Identify power users and set individual ULB overrides — after your first billing cycle, review the AI usage dashboard for users who are consistently blocked, and raise their individual budget.
    3. Set an enterprise spending limit to cap total metered charges once the shared pool is exhausted.
    4. Enable "Stop usage when budget limit is reached" on every spending limit you create — without it, the limit only sends alerts and never actually stops spend.
    5. Monitor monthly — check the AI usage dashboard for users blocked early in the cycle (budget too tight), unexpected metered charges (pool exhausting early), or a clean month with no blocked users (well-sized budgets).
  • This exercise never requires saving a production change — the Admin path always uses a sandbox/test scope, and the Simulation path is a paper worksheet

🖥️ Demo: Setting Up a Bundled AI Credits Budget

  1. Navigate to Enterprise → Billing & licensing → Cost centers, then click New cost center
  2. Under Resources, select the enterprise team that should fund and track the group's usage
  3. Confirm the team is not already assigned to another cost center; reassignment moves the resource
  4. Open Budgets and alerts, then create the appropriate cost-center ULB and metered spending budget
  5. Set the amount — for a universal ULB, set it above the per-license value ($19 Business / $39 Enterprise)
  6. Configure alerts: included-usage alerts fire at 90% and 100%; budget alerts fire at 75%, 90%, and 100%
  7. Where available, enable Stop usage when budget limit is reached to convert the budget into a hard stop

4. Administrative Operating Procedures & Close (20 min)

Key Points

  • A budget configuration is not "set and forget" — it needs a recurring administrative runbook so guardrails stay correctly sized as usage patterns change
  • Monthly cadence should cover: reviewing alert history (included-usage and budget alerts), checking blocked-user counts, reviewing metered overage, and re-validating cost-center included-usage allocations against current headcount
  • An exception/escalation path should be lightweight — a single request form or ticket type — so a legitimate power user or team isn't blocked for days waiting on a manual budget increase

Administrative Runbook (Monthly Cycle)

Step Action Owner
1 Review included-usage alerts (90%/100%) and budget alerts (75%/90%/100%) fired during the prior cycle Billing Manager
2 Pull the AI usage dashboard/export; identify blocked users and which ULB blocked them Platform Admin
3 Reconcile cost-center membership against enterprise teams or IdP groups; confirm headcount and re-validate the included-usage allocation FinOps Lead
4 Review metered overage against the enterprise/org spending budget; confirm hard-stop status is still the intended setting Enterprise/Org Owner
5 Approve or deny any pending exception requests (see below); document the reason either way Billing Manager
6 Communicate any budget changes to affected users/teams before they take effect Platform Admin

🧪 Hands-On: Exception & Escalation Exercise (10 min)

Roles: Admin and Observer work together; rotate who writes the decision.

For each request below, decide: (a) who should approve it, (b) whether it changes a ULB, a cost-center budget, or an enterprise budget, and (c) what you would document as the reason:

  1. A single power user is blocked by their individual ULB three times in one week, but their manager confirms the work is legitimate and time-sensitive.
  2. An IdP-synced enterprise team adds 15 engineers mid-month; the cost center updates automatically but exhausts its included-usage allocation on day 10.
  3. An enterprise-wide spending budget is projected to hit 100% five days before the monthly reset, with hard stop not enabled.
  4. A team requests that their cost center's spending budget hard stop be disabled because a legitimate multi-day agent task keeps getting blocked.

Success Criteria

  • ✅ You correctly route Scenario 1 to an individual ULB increase, approved by a Billing Manager or the user's manager per your escalation policy
  • ✅ You correctly identify Scenario 2 as successful membership automation followed by a cost-center included-usage allocation problem, not a spending-budget problem
  • ✅ You correctly identify Scenario 3 as a case where alert-only behavior means the team must act manually before the reset, since hard stop is not enabled
  • ✅ You can articulate at least one risk of approving Scenario 4 outright versus raising the budget amount instead

Discussion Points

  • Who should own the monthly runbook end-to-end — is it one role or a shared responsibility across Billing Manager, Platform Admin, and FinOps Lead?
  • How would you keep the exception path lightweight without turning it into an unmonitored back door around every guardrail?
  • What would trigger you to revisit your universal ULB or enterprise budget sooner than the next monthly cycle?

Recap

  • AI Credits are simple in principle (1 credit = $0.01, pooled, reset monthly) but the guardrails around them have real nuance — precedence, hard-stop rules, and the included-usage-vs-spending-budget distinction all matter
  • ULBs always hard-stop; cost-center/org/enterprise budgets only hard-stop when explicitly enabled — most incidents trace back to that distinction being misunderstood
  • A monthly administrative runbook, not a one-time setup, is what keeps budgets correctly sized as usage evolves
  • This module stands alone, but Module 2 (developer optimization patterns) and Module 3 (FinOps reporting) build directly on the guardrails established here
