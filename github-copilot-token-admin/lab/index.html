GitHub Copilot AI Credits Optimization — Module 1 Hands-On Lab Guide

Duration: ~63 minutes of hands-on exercises (across a ~115-minute module)
Format: Step-by-step lab exercises with worksheets
Audience: Enterprise/org owners, billing managers, FinOps leaders, platform admins, engineering leaders
Roles used throughout: Admin (drives live UI where available, sandbox/test scope only), Observer (completes worksheets, asks questions, compares results)

Lab Overview

This lab guide contains all the hands-on exercises from Module 1: Administration & Budget Guardrails, part of the GitHub Copilot AI Credits Optimization curriculum. Each lab maps to a workshop section and builds on the previous one. Complete them in order.

This module — and this lab guide — can be delivered standalone. It does not depend on the labs for Module 2 (Developer Token Optimization Patterns, see github-copilot-token-developer-workshop.md) or Module 3 (FinOps Reporting & Chargeback/Showback, see github-copilot-token-finops-workshop.md). For the full curriculum, see github-copilot-token-workshop.md.

Important: No lab in this guide requires making a live paid AI request, and none require saving a production configuration change. Every calculation and configuration can be completed with the worked examples and the supplied worksheets. If you do configure a real budget during Lab 3, enable Stop usage when budget limit is reached only in a sandbox/test scope — a live hard-stop budget can immediately block production users.

Lab 1: Included-Credit Math Check (5 min)

Workshop Section: 1 — AI Credits Billing Fundamentals

Roles

  • Admin and Observer work together; either role can drive the calculation

Reference — Included Credits

Plan Standard (from Sep 1, 2026) Promo (Jun 1 – Sep 1, 2026)
Business 1,900/user/month 3,000/user/month
Enterprise 3,900/user/month 7,000/user/month

Steps

Using the table above, calculate the total monthly pooled credits for a fictional 500-user Copilot Business organization, once during the standard period and once during the promotional period:

  1. Standard period total pool: 500 × ______ = ______
  2. Promotional period total pool: 500 × ______ = ______
  3. Difference between the two totals (in credits, and in USD at $0.01/credit)

Answer Key

  1. Standard period total pool: 500 × 1,900 = 950,000 credits
  2. Promotional period total pool: 500 × 3,000 = 1,500,000 credits
  3. Difference: 1,500,000 − 950,000 = 550,000 credits = 550,000 × $0.01 = $5,500

Success Criteria

  • ✅ You calculated the standard-period pool as 950,000 credits
  • ✅ You calculated the promotional-period pool as 1,500,000 credits
  • ✅ You can state the dollar-value difference ($5,500) and why it matters for September 1, 2026 planning

Lab 2: Scenario Trace — The Billing Flow (8 min)

Workshop Section: 2 — Budget Controls & Billing Flow

Roles

  • Admin and Observer work together; either role can lead the trace out loud

Reference Diagram

┌────────────────┐ ┌────────────────┐ ┌────────────────┐ ┌────────────────┐ ┌────────────────┐
│      User      │ │  User budget   │ │     Shared     │ │   Paid usage   │ │Spending budget │
│    request     │►│     check      │►│      pool      │►│     policy     │►│  / hard stop   │
└────────────────┘ └────────────────┘ └────────────────┘ └────────────────┘ └────────────────┘
  1. User budget check — most specific active ULB (individual > cost-center > universal). A $0 or exhausted ULB always blocks — no fallback.
  2. Shared pool — served with no additional metered charge beyond the license fee while the pool (org/enterprise, resets monthly at 00:00 UTC, no rollover) has credits.
  3. Paid usage policy — once the pool is exhausted, the "AI credits paid usage" policy (default: enabled) decides whether metered usage begins.
  4. Spending budget / hard stop — cost-center/org/enterprise budget hard-stops only if "Stop usage when budget limit is reached" is enabled (off by default); otherwise it's alert-only.

Steps

For each scenario, write down where the request is blocked (if at all) and why:

  1. A user with a $0 individual ULB sends a Chat prompt. The org pool still has 40% of its credits remaining.
  2. A user with no individual override sends a prompt. The shared pool is fully exhausted, and the enterprise has disabled the "AI credits paid usage" policy.
  3. A user with no individual override sends a prompt. The shared pool is exhausted, paid usage is enabled, and the enterprise budget has hit 100% with hard stop not enabled.
  4. Same as #3, but hard stop is enabled on the enterprise budget.

Answer Key

  1. Blocked at Step 1 (user-level budget check) — a $0 ULB always hard-stops the user regardless of pool state; there is no fallback.
  2. Blocked at the paid usage policy check — once the pool is exhausted, a disabled "AI credits paid usage" policy blocks all further usage until the next billing cycle.
  3. Allowed (metered charge applied) — the enterprise budget is at 100% but has no hard stop enabled, so it functions as an alert only; usage continues and is billed as overage.
  4. Blocked at Step 4 (applicable spending budget) — with hard stop enabled, reaching 100% of the enterprise budget blocks further metered usage.

Success Criteria

  • ✅ You correctly identified Scenario 1 as blocked at the user-level budget step, regardless of pool state
  • ✅ You correctly identified Scenario 2 as blocked at the paid usage policy step
  • ✅ You correctly identified Scenario 3 as allowed (alert only, no hard stop configured)
  • ✅ You correctly identified Scenario 4 as blocked at the spending budget step

Lab 3: Budget Design Worksheet (20 min)

Workshop Section: 3 — Hands-On Budget Configuration

Roles

  • Admin: Drives the live Billing & Licensing UI (sandbox/test scope only — never saved to production)
  • Observer: Completes the Budget Design Worksheet in parallel

Quick reference: Use the two-slide Power-User Budget Override visual when selecting an individual ULB. Its worked example shows that the individual amount replaces, rather than adds to, the universal and cost-center limits.

Reference — Enterprise Teams as the Membership Layer

Capability Administrative Impact
Assign an enterprise team to a cost center Every current team member is added to the cost center, and cost-center membership updates automatically as people join or leave the team
Assign Copilot Business licenses directly Copilot access follows enterprise-team membership independently of organization membership
Sync an enterprise team with an IdP group (EMU) Membership changes flow from the identity provider through SCIM; an IdP-managed enterprise team cannot also contain manually assigned users
Enforce single cost-center ownership An organization, repository, user, or enterprise team can belong to only one cost center; assigning it elsewhere moves it to the new cost center

Walk through the fields (Budget Type → Bundled AI credits budget → scope → amount → alerts → stop option) without saving a production change — use a designated sandbox/test scope only.
  • Simulation path: Skip directly to Step 2 and fill in the worksheet as if you were configuring the UI.

  • ✅ You can explain the difference between a cost center's included-usage allocation and its spending budget
  • ✅ You can explain how enterprise-team membership automates cost-center membership without automatically sizing the budgets

Lab 4: Exception & Escalation Exercise (10 min)

Workshop Section: 4 — Administrative Operating Procedures & Close

Roles

  • Admin and Observer work together; rotate who writes the decision

Reference — Administrative Runbook (Monthly Cycle)

Step Action Owner
1 Review included-usage alerts (90%/100%) and budget alerts (75%/90%/100%) fired during the prior cycle Billing Manager
2 Pull the AI usage dashboard/export; identify blocked users and which ULB blocked them Platform Admin
3 Reconcile cost-center membership against enterprise teams or IdP groups; confirm headcount and re-validate the included-usage allocation FinOps Lead
4 Review metered overage against the enterprise/org spending budget; confirm hard-stop status is still the intended setting Enterprise/Org Owner
5 Approve or deny any pending exception requests (see below); document the reason either way Billing Manager
6 Communicate any budget changes to affected users/teams before they take effect Platform Admin

Steps

For each request below, decide: (a) who should approve it, (b) whether it changes a ULB, a cost-center budget, or an enterprise budget, and (c) what you would document as the reason:

  1. A single power user is blocked by their individual ULB three times in one week, but their manager confirms the work is legitimate and time-sensitive.
  2. An IdP-synced enterprise team adds 15 engineers mid-month; the cost center updates automatically but exhausts its included-usage allocation on day 10.
  3. An enterprise-wide spending budget is projected to hit 100% five days before the monthly reset, with hard stop not enabled.
  4. A team requests that their cost center's spending budget hard stop be disabled because a legitimate multi-day agent task keeps getting blocked.

Fill in your worksheet:

Request Approver Control Changed Documented Reason
1
2
3
4

Answer Key

Request Approver Control Changed Documented Reason
1 Billing Manager (or per your escalation policy, the user's manager co-signs) Individual ULB increase Legitimate, time-sensitive work confirmed by the user's manager; raise the individual override above the universal ULB
2 FinOps Lead Cost-center included-usage allocation (not the spending budget) Enterprise-team membership automation worked, but headcount grew mid-cycle; re-baseline the allocation rather than changing the spending budget
3 Enterprise/Org Owner No control change yet — this is an alert-only condition since hard stop is not enabled Team must decide manually whether to raise the enterprise budget or accept the projected overage before reset, since the system will not stop it automatically
4 Enterprise/Org Owner or Billing Manager, with documented risk acceptance Cost-center budget hard-stop toggle Balance the legitimate multi-day task against the risk of uncapped overage; consider raising the budget amount instead of disabling hard stop outright

Success Criteria

  • ✅ You correctly routed Scenario 1 to an individual ULB increase, approved per your escalation policy
  • ✅ You correctly identified Scenario 2 as successful membership automation followed by a cost-center included-usage allocation problem, not a spending-budget problem
  • ✅ You correctly identified Scenario 3 as a case where alert-only behavior means the team must act manually before the reset
  • ✅ You articulated at least one risk of approving Scenario 4 outright versus raising the budget amount instead
